Nature, in the broadest sense, is the physical world or universe.
 In this general sense nature refers to the laws, elements and phenomena 
 of the physical world, including life. Although humans are part of nature, 
 human activity or humans as a whole are often described as at times at odds,
  or outright separate and even superior to nature.[1]

During the advent of modern scientific method 
in the last several centuries, nature became the passive reality,
 organized and moved by divine laws.[2][3] With the Industrial Revolution,
  nature increasingly became seen as the part of reality not subject to intentional 
  human intervention. It was considered sacred by some traditions
   (Rousseau, American transcendentalism) but a mere decorum for divine providence
    or human history (Hegel, Marx) in others. However, a vitalist vision of nature, 
    closer to the pre-Socratic one, was reborn at the same time, especially after
     Charles Darwin.[1]
